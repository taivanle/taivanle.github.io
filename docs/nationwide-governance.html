Making governance part of the workflow.

An enterprise pilot translating policy and risk criteria into structured inputs, approval gates, and traceable lifecycle decisions.

The challenge

Governance policies describe requirements, but delivery systems still need an interpretable way to decide what can happen next. In the Nationwide pilot, the work was to turn risk and compliance intent into structured workflow constraints.

System approach

01Risk metadata
02Tier & required controls
03Evidence & approval

Conceptual flow · simplified to explain the engineering approach.

THE DESIGN DECISION

Keep the rule and the rationale connected. A reviewer should be able to trace a required control back to the structured input that triggered it, with proportionate requirements for lower-risk paths.

What I built

  1. Defined metadata schemas and risk-tier inputs, separating customer impact from data sensitivity so reviewers could understand what triggered a control.
  2. Mapped risk tiers to conditional workflow paths, mandatory evidence, and approval checkpoints, with state transitions that constrained lifecycle progression.
  3. Worked across orchestration and enterprise content services, including watsonx, MCP interfaces, FileNet on OpenShift, and supporting metadata and artefact storage.

Scope & reflection

This is a qualitative pilot case study based on the existing public portfolio account. It describes engineering contributions and the integration approach, not an independent compliance certification or a quantified customer outcome.

What I take forward

Governance becomes useful to engineering teams when policy is expressed as clear inputs, enforceable transitions, and evidence they can inspect.
