The challenge

Evaluating model-generated artefacts needs more than a plausible-sounding answer. The grader has to assess correctness against expected outputs and explicit criteria, while keeping repeated benchmarking affordable and reproducible.

THE DESIGN DECISION

Different criteria need different graders. Combining deterministic checks with rubric-guided model assessment creates a clearer boundary between what can be verified directly and what requires judgement.

What I built

  1. Designed and implemented Themis as a hybrid grading pipeline: deterministic validation for machine-checkable properties, rubric-based assessment, and LLM judgement for the remaining criteria.
  2. Built custom evaluation environments that execute grading workflows end to end and compare model outputs with golden datasets and structured rubrics.
  3. Automated repeatable benchmarking across multiple models and compared the approach with Gandalf the Grader and LLM-as-a-Judge methods.

Scope & reflection

Results are reported in my resume for the evaluation workflows used at Aptura. They describe that benchmark context; they are not a general claim about model accuracy. This case study summarises my contribution without publishing employer code or datasets.

What I take forward

Evaluation is part of the engineering system. Cost, reproducibility, and the ability to explain a score matter alongside the score itself.
